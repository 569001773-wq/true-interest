What is True Interest?

True Interest is a free student loan calculator that shows the real cost of borrowing, not just the monthly payment. Most loan offers highlight a low monthly number, but over 10 or 20 years the interest can add up to more than you originally borrowed. This tool does the math instantly so you can see the total you will actually pay back before you sign.

How the student loan cost calculator works

Enter your loan amount, interest rate, and repayment term. True Interest uses standard loan amortization, the same formula lenders use, to estimate your monthly payment, total amount paid, and the extra cost from interest. The Reality Check compares your payment to typical U.S. full-time pay so you can see how big a bite it takes.

Every number is a clear estimate for education only. It is not financial, legal, tax, or loan servicing advice; always confirm with your school, lender, or loan servicer before signing.

Frequently asked questions

How much will my student loan really cost?

It depends on your loan amount, interest rate, and repayment term. Enter them above and True Interest shows your estimated total paid and how much of it is interest.

Is this student loan calculator free?

Yes. The web calculator is completely free. The iPhone app adds document scanning and a full plain-English report as an optional one-time purchase.

Does it work for private and federal student loans?

Yes. It works for any fixed-rate loan where you know the amount, rate, and term, private or federal. For loans with changing rates or staged payments, treat the result as a simplified estimate.

What does True Interest mean?

True Interest is the extra money you pay on top of what you borrow, the total interest over the life of the loan. It is the number most loan offers do not show you up front.

True Interest is an educational tool. This is an estimate, not financial, legal, tax, or loan servicing advice. Check with your school, lender, or loan servicer before signing. Typical pay is the U.S. median for full-time workers (Bureau of Labor Statistics), before taxes.
